Doctrine 01 · Conformance

Every pack, every case

The doctrine is executable, and split three ways: _doctrine/doctrine.js is the engine, _doctrine/fixtures.js is the case matrix, _doctrine/tests.js holds the assertions. Each template ships its own copy pack and may add its own checks. This card is a renderer for those tests, not a second harness — node templates/_doctrine/run-checks.js loads the same four files and prints the same results, exiting non-zero on any failure.

This is a presentation doctrine, not the security boundary

Everything asserted here decides what a permitted viewer is shown. It does not decide what they are permitted to receive. Unauthorized rows, cells, counts, pagination metadata and exports must be filtered or authorized server-side, before the payload reaches the client. Omitting a node from the DOM, dropping a column, and hiding an element with CSS are not access control — the data is still in the response, and the response is readable. A green verdict means the screen states the right amount; it says nothing about what was sent.

Classification

Orthogonal axes

The first guard banned standalone 0–3 and a word list below Service — that mistakes spelling for meaning, since "2 reservations" is ordinary product data and service is ordinary English. The second fixed provenance but conflated three questions into one tag: meta was provenance, text/datum were presentation forms, secret was a disclosure policy. So text silently meant no minimum, and prose could carry a Ledger fact past the guard while a harmless integer was blocked for looking like one.

Each axis is now declared independently. Classification describes what a fragment is; policy decides separately whether it may exist, be disclosed, and be acted upon. A datum may be secret, and prose may require Ledger.

Declaration is also separated from selection. A pack lists its fragments once in a catalogue, with policy that cannot see the context; select(ctx) then returns declared IDs or an explicit none. That is what makes the policy class non-vacuous — while branches built their own fragments inline, a minimum could be derived from the same ctx.exposure the branch was keyed on, so the guard could not fail by construction. Class B now forces every catalogued fragment through dispose() at every context with no selector involved.

Packs never receive a denial reason. decide() keeps the exact reason for the audit; a pack sees only a disposition — render, fallback, omit. When a fragment's existence may not be acknowledged, missing capability, insufficient exposure and true absence all collapse to the same disposition, so a refusal cannot be differenced into an enumeration oracle. The collapse is the default inside one function, never a branch a pack opts into.

kind
What it is

prose · datum · action · label. A presentation form only — it grants and withholds nothing on its own.

provenance
Where it came from

doctrine · domain. Doctrine metadata is the ladder counting itself, and never renders below Service whatever its kind.

minExposure
When it may be shown

0–3, and a property of the content, not of the branch that selected it — otherwise the check is unfalsifiable.

existence
When it may be admitted

public may be acknowledged as withheld; conditional only at or above its existenceAt ceiling; secret is omitted whole and never acknowledged.

capability
What you must hold

Checked before exposure, because holding no grant is a harder no than standing too low — and must not be reported as an exposure problem.

fallback
What shows instead

Per field, per pack. A refusal degrades that one field to a safe line and fails conformance separately; it never blanks critical UI.

Unclassified text is itself a violation: a pack returning a bare string gets its field-specific fallback and fails conformance. Lexical scanning survives only as secondary lint — advisory, never part of a verdict, and with no digit rule.

Fallbacks

A violation never blanks the screen

Required fields always render something coherent. When a projector fails — missing value, unclassified string, a fragment above the viewer's exposure — the field's own fallback renders and conformance fails separately. Optional fields omit silently, which is the designed behaviour rather than a fault.

Rows, columns and affordances resolved per role at the console. Hidden records are absent, not masked; aggregates are computed over the visible set only; export is a separate grant at every level.
