FREESIDEDoctrine 01 · Exposure

The machine
is dressed,
not hidden

Freeside is a resort wrapped around a machine built for profit. The garden is a life-support system wearing a garden costume, and the costume is honest — on the terrace the machinery is visible, elegant, and simply not labelled. Nothing is concealed. It is undescribed.

Every screen in this system sits somewhere on one scale: how much of the machine is showing. That number is called exposure. It is the only mechanism in the doctrine, and everything else — register, pair, density, wording — is downstream of it.

Not a mood

Exposure is computed, not chosen. A designer never picks "cyberpunk here". They state depth, access and state; the register follows.

Not a theme toggle

There is no dark mode. Deep Space is not night — it is the service level. Paradise is not day — it is the guest level.

Not decoration

Higher exposure means more arithmetic on screen, not more texture. No grime, no hazard tape, no distress. The underneath is clean.

The mechanism

Two dials and a ceiling

Depth sets what the task normally requires. State can force more structure to appear, for diagnosis or recovery. Access does neither — it is the ceiling on how far the interface may disclose. Elevated access permits deeper views; it never activates them.

exposure = min( access ceiling, max( depth baseline, state floor ) )

The asymmetry is the point. State pushes up from below and access caps from above, so a failing system can never widen disclosure. A guest watching the station fail still sees the terrace — the resort does not show its machinery to guests, least of all when the machinery is what is failing.

On the pairs

The pair ladder is not arbitrary. resort is the sun on concrete. garden is the life-support costume. systems is the clinical machine, cold and competent. vice is the house edge, and it appears only where money is being taken.

On nesting

A subtree may re-enter a register: data-register="deep" inside a light page renders a Service panel correctly without hex literals. Use it for the seam — a raised-exposure fragment quoted inside a lower-exposure page. Never use it to put two registers side by side for contrast.

On dynasty

The fifth pair sits outside the ladder. dynasty is ownership — Tessier-Ashpool itself. Use it for governance, provenance and anything that speaks as the house rather than about it. Never mix it into a level.

Non-negotiable

Three laws

01
One underlying truth, progressively disclosed

The substrate is identical at every level; what changes is how much of it is disclosed. Terrace need not surface every fact the Ledger holds — but nothing at Terrace may contradict it. If the terrace says "included" and the ledger says "4.2% to the house", both are true and the guest was not misled. If a level needs a different fact to hold, the design is lying and the level is wrong.

02
The machine is dressed, not hidden

Surface machinery stays visible. A gauge on the terrace is a real gauge reading a real number, quantized to what the terrace may know: three positions, no units, no figure. Never fake an instrument, never draw a system that does not exist, and never hide one behind an illustration.

Every channel is a disclosure channel. Text, numerals, geometry, ordering, colour, animation, accessibility metadata and DOM structure all represent the same facts, and each must stay within the precision the level permits. A bar whose width is the reading states the reading, whatever the caption beside it says.

03
State cannot breach the ceiling

Depth and state raise exposure; access caps it. A failure may force structure to appear so it can be diagnosed, but it may never widen disclosure — an alert is not an authorisation. Where the floor exceeds the ceiling the screen clamps: it acknowledges without detailing. What a clamp may say is set by the disclosure mode, not by the level alone.

04
Agency tracks role

Two users may receive equally honest accounts of the same state and still be offered different things to do about it. A guest reads "ask at the desk"; a member reads "request access"; an operator reads "request elevation"; a principal reads "open on record". The description is shared; the affordance is not. Never render an action the role cannot complete — a dead button is a worse disclosure than a plain refusal.

Availability and presentation are separate concerns. Role and capability decide whether an action may be performed; the resource's existence policy decides whether it may be offered at all; exposure governs only how specifically it is labelled and explained; and the destination resolves its own exposure under the same access ceiling. An operator on the terrace is still offered operations — below Service it is simply called "the console" rather than named by sector. Availability must never depend on exposure alone; presentation may.

The three copy laws, together
Severity tracks state

Degraded and offline are different words. A guest during an alert hears a calm sentence — that is under-disclosure, not contradiction.

Specificity tracks exposure

How much working is shown. No exposure integer and no ladder vocabulary appears below Service, in any string, ever.

Agency tracks role

What you may do about it. Actions are projected from the role, never from the outcome alone. Availability never depends on exposure; the wording may.

These are three axes, not one. Conflating them is what produces a screen that softens the severity word and then prints the machine's own counting underneath it.

Clamp policy

What a refusal may admit

A clamp is not one behaviour. Whether it may acknowledge hidden handling depends on the resource, not the screen — so sensitivity is declared on the resource and the mode is derived. The governing rule: a clamp may acknowledge that hidden handling exists, but it must not confirm a sensitive object's existence or disclose the shape of the inaccessible layer.

Silent
Do not confirm existence

The screen reads as though the destination is not a thing here. No acknowledgment, no name, no shape. For sensitive resources whose existence is above the asker's ceiling.

Abstract
Admit handling, not contents

Something is being attended to and it is not yours. No structure, no identifiers, no counting. The normal Terrace and Atrium clamp.

Explicit
Name the withheld layer

The asker already knows the layer exists, so the refusal states which, at what ceiling, and that it was recorded. Service and above.

Failure modes

Not this

Every one of these has been produced by a well-intentioned generator working from the palette alone. They are the reason this page exists.

Cyberpunk as texture

Scanlines, glitch, terminal green, monospace everywhere, hazard chevrons. The underneath is clinical — cold, well-lit, competent. Nothing on Freeside is broken; it is simply not for guests.

Solarpunk as illustration

Drawn leaves, vine borders, hand-lettered warmth. The garden is infrastructure. It reads as light, water, concrete and space — not as ornament applied to a UI.

Elevated access as a theme

An operator signs in and the whole product turns dark. Access permits depth; it does not apply it. An operator reading the welcome page is on the terrace like everybody else.

Both registers on one screen for contrast

A dark hero above a light body is not the duality. It is a gradient with extra steps. One screen resolves to one exposure.

Lore in the information architecture

Navigation reads "Members", "Settlement", "Access" — not "Villa Straylight". Gibson supplies the logic, not the labels. Flavour lives in transitions, empty states, and the words around the data.

Density as seriousness

A roster is dense because it holds 4,000 rows, not because density looks professional. Exposure sets density; content does not get to vote.

The test

Name the depth, the access and the state. If those three do not produce the screen in front of you, the screen is decorated rather than designed.
