There is no horizon here

Freeside is a spindle and you stand on the inside of it. The ground curves up at both sides and closes overhead, the light is a tube down the axis, the far side of the habitat hangs above you as haze. That place arrives as a render or a photograph. The system around it is deliberately thin: sky behind, one arc closing overhead, and the overlays that seat the image in the page.

Plate
The render or photograph. The only place the place appears — everything else is scaffolding.
Grid
Survey overlay. Invisible at Terrace and Atrium, full at Service, two thirds at Ledger.
Axis
Lado-Acheson, for frames with no plate. One hard line and one bloom, never a sun disc. A render carries its own axis in perspective, so the two are never combined.
Curve
A single wide arc with a hairline edge, implying the habitat closing overhead. One arc, not a panorama.
Scrim
Top light-wash, added only where ink text sits over the plate.
Veil
The bottom feather into the page surface. No hard photo edge ever meets content.
Order
Plate, grid, axis, curve, scrim, veil, annotation. Every layer is optional; a plate and a veil is a legitimate composition.

An earlier version of this file built the whole panorama in CSS — terraces, canal, crowd marks, an inverted far side. It read as an airport diagram. Gradients cannot carry luxury architecture, and a diagram of paradise is worse than a frame waiting for a picture. The geometry now lives in the image.

Scaffolding aloneWith no plate the frame is still a place: fabricated sky, the axis, the arc overhead. Use it where a render would be too much.
PlatesWhat the image owes the composition
Yes

Fabricated blue sky. Linear axial light. Ground and terraces curving up at the frame edges. Cultivated planting against poured concrete. Water or mist. Guests, staff, a tender — evidence that the place is worked. Machinery as elegant seams and service traces.

No

Wilderness. A flat Earth horizon. Rust, damp, ruin. Empty spa minimalism with one plant. Neon, rain-slick streets, holograms. Windows onto space, and the station seen from outside.

Implemented in environment.css, imported by styles.css. In a template the plate is an <image-slot> so the render can be replaced by dropping a file on it; in a specimen it is a plain <img>. Add .fs-env-band for the horizon-strip proportion used inside a page, and .fs-env-scrim wherever ink text sits over the plate. Annotations are permitted only where the level permits them, and in a product they must be projected through Exposure like any other copy — never typed into the markup.
