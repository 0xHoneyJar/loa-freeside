How standing is scored

Every member carries one score, recomputed when an epoch closes. This document explains what feeds it, what it is used for, and what happens when it falls.

How scoring works

Your score is a weighted sum of three dimensions, each measured independently and none of them able to carry the whole figure alone. A member strong in one dimension and absent in the others lands mid-range by design — the score rewards breadth of participation rather than a single kind of commitment.

Weights are fixed when an epoch opens and cannot be changed while it runs. This is deliberate: a score that could be reweighted mid-epoch would be a score nobody could plan against.

The three dimensions

Standing measures how long you have been here and how consistently. It moves slowly and is difficult to acquire quickly, which is the point.

Collection measures depth rather than volume. Holding a great many of one thing reads as less than holding across the range.

Activity measures onchain participation in the current epoch, and is the only dimension that can move sharply in either direction.

Epochs and weighting

An epoch closes on a schedule, not on demand. At close, the roster is snapshotted, dimensions are recomputed, and the new scores commit together. Nothing is written between close and commit, so a score you read mid-close is the previous epoch's and is labelled as such.

Losing standing

Scores decay when activity stops, and the decay is gradual and reversible. What is not reversible is a flag: an account flagged for wash activity is removed from scoring entirely and is reviewed by a person rather than a system.
